SOURCES TRIED AND DROPPED
- Bastani et al. 2025, PNAS, "Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning" (DOI 10.1073/pnas.2422633122). Verified, but I dropped it from sources because the participants were high-school students, which the population rule excludes. It is still the clearest causal evidence for AI-as-crutch harm: 17% lower grades once access was removed, and a tutor-style AI mostly avoided that harm. Worth knowing about.
- Barbieri et al. 2023, worked-examples meta-analysis (DOI 10.1007/s10648-023-09745-1). Crossref confirms it exists. I could not read the abstract: Crossref and Semantic Scholar had none, and the Springer page returned a WebFetch proxy 429 (rate limit). It also covers mathematics only. There is no separate worked-examples action; GROW-04 (self-explanation) covers studying examples.
- Kushlev & Dunn 2015 email-checking dose: the abstract is not available, and the ScienceDirect PII I guessed was the wrong article. The "checking less often reduces stress" claim is kept with no dose.

GAPS / WEAK EVIDENCE
- Extensive listening (GROW-09): I verified no meta-analysis. The action rests on the official format plus the testing effect, labelled Expert consensus.
- The weekly IELTS rotation (GROW-12) is a planning template built from spacing and testing principles, not a tested schedule. The same goes for the "one active course" rule (GROW-14), which is inferred from low MOOC completion rates (Reich 2019) and was never tested as an intervention.
- IELTS Writing's Task 1 20 min / Task 2 40 min split: the fetched ielts.org page confirmed only 60 minutes in total, so the split is marked as a planning default.
- Deliberate practice explains little variance in professions (<1%) or education (4%) (Macnamara 2014), so there is weak evidence for "practice hours" as the main lever in UX careers.
- Portfolio/case-study building has no experimental evidence. It is supported only by the Google certificate's official structure (three portfolio projects).
- Interview practice was not tested on its own; the support comes from intervention components in Liu 2014.
- Time blocking has no direct RCT. The time-management evidence (Aeon 2021) is mostly correlational.
- The feedback meta-analyses for L2 writing and speaking are classroom or lab based. Self-study with AI feedback is an extrapolation.
- No doses exist for vocabulary words per day, reading minutes, or procrastination and implementation-intention use.

SURPRISING / HONEST CAVEATS
- Phone "out of sight" evidence is mixed. Ward 2017 did not replicate in a preregistered direct replication (Ruiz Pardo & Minda 2022). Parry 2023 (k=56) found only a small working-memory effect; Böttger 2023 found an overall negative effect with high heterogeneity. The action is kept because it costs little, but it is framed as reducing interruptions.
- Turning notifications off completely was worse than batching them (more anxiety and FOMO) in Fitz 2019. Batching 3x/day was the effective arm.
- Social media reduction: Ferguson's meta-analysis found about zero effect, while the reanalysis found small benefits only for interventions lasting 1 week or more and small harms for shorter ones. Limiting use beats abrupt short abstinence.
- Interleaving hurt learning of plain word lists (g = -0.39 for words in Brunmair 2019), so it should not be applied to IELTS vocabulary lists.
- Micro-breaks improved vigor and reduced fatigue, but did not significantly improve performance on cognitively demanding tasks.
- Job-search interventions raised employment odds 2.67x, but only when they combined skill-building with motivation. Overall search intensity did not predict job quality.
- Weak ties: moderately weak ties produced the most job mobility (an inverted U), and weak ties helped most in digital industries, which is relevant to UX.
- Tamheer eligibility (per HRDF): Saudi nationals aged 30 or under, at most one year of experience, currently unemployed, 3-6 months of training, SAR 3,000 monthly allowance. Re-check before applying.
